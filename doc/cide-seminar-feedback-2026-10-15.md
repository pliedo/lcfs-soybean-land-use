# CIDE seminar feedback: questions for follow-up

Seminar: CIDE, October 15, 2026  
Recorded: October 20, 2026  
Source: questions reported by Pedro Liedo Orozco after the presentation.

These are audience questions, not established findings or resolved methodological decisions. All 14 questions are retained below, grouped by topic. Checkboxes track preparation of a documented response.

## Policy design and transmission

- [ ] **Q01 — Other biofuel policies.** What about the policies regarding the minimum share of biofuel in gasoline, for example?
- [ ] **Q02 — Incentive mechanism.** How does the subsidy work? Is it a direct subsidy?
- [ ] **Q03 — Approval process.** How long does the application process take? What percentage of applications are approved, and how much funding is provided?
- [ ] **Q04 — Supply-chain pass-through.** What are the connections between the different agents involved, particularly regarding the pass-through of the subsidy?

## Data and exposure construction

- [ ] **Q05 — Panel and facility entry.** Is it a panel dataset? Which years does it cover? Does it include facilities established after the policy was introduced?
- [ ] **Q06 — Transport distance.** What are the usual distance measures used in the literature? Why did you choose this particular distance measure?
- [ ] **Q07 — Crusher capacity.** Do you have data on the processing capacity of crushers? Could you combine this information with distance to construct a capacity-weighted measure?

## Identification and prices

- [ ] **Q08 — Price effects.** What about the effect on price?
- [ ] **Q09 — Expected soybean prices and fixed effects.** What about future soybean prices? How does the fixed effect deal with that?
- [ ] **Q10 — Selection into application.** The application process is not random. How does your identification strategy address this?
- [ ] **Q11 — Endogeneity.** How does your identification strategy deal with endogeneity?

## Outcomes and broader implications

- [ ] **Q12 — Land allocation versus productivity.** You focus on the share of land rather than on productivity. Why?
- [ ] **Q13 — Food versus fuel.** What about the discussion surrounding the food-versus-fuel debate?
- [ ] **Q14 — Rotation and soil quality.** Could changes in crop rotation improve soil quality?

## Notes for developing responses

- Distinguish LCFS credits and market incentives from direct government subsidies or application funding when answering Q02–Q04; the wording above preserves the questions.
- Separate policy approval, refinery entry, and physical capacity expansion.
- For Q09–Q11, distinguish common price movements absorbed by year effects from local or exposure-correlated expected-price changes; fixed effects alone do not resolve selection or endogeneity.
- For Q07, investigate data availability and measurement before adopting a crusher-capacity allocation rule.
- For Q12–Q14, distinguish observed planting patterns, yields, and soil outcomes; descriptive crop histories do not by themselves establish productivity or soil-quality effects.
